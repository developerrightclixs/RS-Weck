The Value of Experience

The advantage of hiring a seasoned commercial real estate appraiser lies in the ability to recognize, analyze, and interpret the many factors that influence a valuation outcome—particularly those that a less experienced appraiser might overlook.

Throughout my career, I have prepared thousands of commercial real estate appraisals, been deposed numerous times, and qualified as an expert witness in both Sacramento Superior Court and Federal Bankruptcy Court.

My investigative training while serving in the Enforcement Division of the Bureau of Real Estate Appraisers (BREA) further strengthened my ability to critically evaluate appraisal reports, with particular attention to the underlying assumptions, analytical methods, and valuation methodologies that support a property's concluded value.

Combining my analytical tools through the disciplined application of the Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice (USPAP) allows me to easily identify errors, unsupported assumptions, analytical inconsistencies, and other weaknesses that may materially affect a valuation conclusion.

Experience matters—not simply in determining value, but in understanding how that value was developed, whether the analysis is adequately supported, and where weaknesses may exist.

About us

Professional Experience

The MAI designation is widely recognized as one of the most prestigious professional credentials in the real estate appraisal industry. Awarded by the Appraisal Institute, it identifies professionals who have demonstrated advanced knowledge, specialized experience, and a commitment to ethical and professional standards in real estate valuation and investment analysis. The designation is recognized by courts, government agencies, financial institutions, attorneys, and real estate investors as a mark of excellence.

Commercial real estate valuation often requires far more than identifying comparable sales and applying standard valuation techniques. It requires the ability to recognize, analyze, and interpret the many factors that influence value, including market conditions, income and expenses, capitalization rates, highest and best use, financing considerations, and the economic characteristics of the property.

An experienced MAI appraiser brings a higher level of analytical discipline to these assignments. This can be especially important in litigation, partnership disputes, estate matters, bankruptcy proceedings, investment decisions, and other situations in which the credibility and defensibility of a valuation are critical.

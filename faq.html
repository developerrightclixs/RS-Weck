The Appraisal Institute (AI) is the largest global professional association for real property appraisers, established in 1991 through the unification of the American Institute of Real Estate Appraisers (AIREA) and the Society of Real Estate Appraisers. The organization serves as a primary credentialing body, advocacy group, and educational provider for real estate valuation professionals across the globe.

The MAI designation is a prestigious professional credential awarded by the Appraisal Institute to real estate appraisers who specialize in the valuation of commercial and other types of real property. Holders of the designation are qualified to provide opinions of value, appraisal reviews, consulting services, and investment-related valuation analyses for a wide range of property types, including commercial, industrial, agricultural, residential, and vacant land.

To earn the MAI designation, candidates generally must:

  • Meet education prerequisites and complete advanced appraisal coursework.
  • Pass required examinations, including the General Comprehensive Exam.
  • Complete a demonstration of knowledge requirement.
  • Document 4,500 hours of specialized appraisal experience.
  • Adhere to the Appraisal Institute's ethical and professional standards.

The MAI designation is widely recognized by courts, government agencies, financial institutions, and investors as a mark of excellence in commercial real estate valuation and analysis.

The Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice establishes the professional standards governing appraisal practice and imposes detailed requirements that can challenge even experienced appraisal professionals.

Restricted Appraisal Report – A streamlined real estate or asset valuation document defined by the Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice (USPAP) that states the appraiser's final value conclusions with minimal narrative description, explanation, or supporting rationale.

Full Narrative Report – The most comprehensive and detailed report type, containing all the data, background information, and reasoning used by the appraiser so the reader does not need any outside resources to understand the conclusion.
